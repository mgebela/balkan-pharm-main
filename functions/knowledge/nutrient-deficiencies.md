# Nutrient deficiencies and toxicities (cannabis / hemp)

General rule: mobile nutrients (N, P, K, Mg) show symptoms on **older, lower leaves first**,
because the plant relocates them to new growth. Immobile nutrients (Ca, S, Fe, B) show on
**new growth at the top first**, because the plant can't move them once used.
Always check pH before treating as a deficiency — locked-out nutrients look identical to
missing ones. Soil target pH 6.0–7.0; coco/hydro/soilless target pH 5.5–6.5.

## Nitrogen (N) — mobile
- Symptoms: uniform pale yellowing starting on lower/older leaves, working upward. Leaves may
  dry and drop. Growth slows.
- Common cause: underfeeding, or heavy flowering-stage bloom nutrients with too little N late
  flower (some yellowing in late flower is normal senescence, not a deficiency).
- Fix: increase a balanced or veg-leaning nutrient at next feeding. Don't overcorrect — N
  excess causes dark, clawed, overly lush leaves and delays flowering.

## Phosphorus (P) — mobile
- Symptoms: dark green/blue-green leaves, purple or red stems and leaf undersides (also
  genetic in some strains, so don't rely on color alone), small dark spots on lower leaves,
  slowed growth.
- Common cause: cold root zone (P uptake drops below ~18°C / 65°F root temp), pH out of range.
- Fix: correct root-zone temperature first, then check pH before adding more P.

## Potassium (K) — mobile
- Symptoms: yellowing/browning at leaf **edges and tips** ("leaf margin burn") on lower/older
  leaves, weak stems, reduced bud density.
- Common cause: often shows late flower under heavy demand; also a classic overwatering symptom.
- Fix: verify watering schedule and drainage before increasing K.

## Magnesium (Mg) — mobile
- Symptoms: **interveinal chlorosis** — yellowing between veins while veins stay green — on
  lower/mid leaves. Very common in soil grows using RO/soft water.
- Fix: cal-mag supplement or Epsom salt (1 tsp/gallon) as a foliar spray or root drench.

## Calcium (Ca) — immobile
- Symptoms: distorted, curled, or spotted **new growth**; brown spots on young leaves; weak
  stems. Often paired with Mg deficiency (cal-mag products treat both).
- Common cause: RO/soft water with no added minerals, or pH too low for uptake.
- Fix: cal-mag supplement, check pH is not below range.

## Iron (Fe) — immobile
- Symptoms: interveinal chlorosis on **new growth** (top of plant), veins stay green, can go
  nearly white in severe cases.
- Common cause: almost always a high-pH lockout, not true iron absence.
- Fix: correct pH before adding iron — adding more iron won't help if pH is the real problem.

## Sulfur (S) — semi-mobile
- Symptoms: overall pale yellowing, but starts on **new growth**, unlike nitrogen. Less common.

## Nutrient burn / toxicity (over-feeding)
- Symptoms: dark green leaves with **yellow/brown tips and edges curling down** ("clawing"),
  usually on newer growth, but can affect the whole plant if severe.
- Fix: flush with plain pH-balanced water, resume feeding at a lower strength.

## Before treating anything as a deficiency
1. Check and correct pH first — most "deficiencies" in home grows are lockouts from pH drift.
2. Check watering consistency — both over- and under-watering mimic several deficiencies.
3. Check root-zone temperature if symptoms don't match a feeding change.
4. Only then adjust nutrients, and change one variable at a time so you can tell what worked.
