# Common pests and diseases (cannabis / hemp) — identification and IPM-first treatment

Philosophy: identify correctly before treating — many treatments only work on specific pests
and wasting a cycle on the wrong one lets the real problem spread. Prefer the least aggressive,
least toxic option first (IPM = Integrated Pest Management), especially anything close to
harvest, since sprays need time to clear before flowers are trimmed/consumed.

## Spider mites
- Look: fine white/yellow speckling ("stippling") on leaf tops, tiny moving dots on leaf
  undersides with a hand lens, fine webbing in severe infestations (usually near colas).
- Cause: thrives in hot, dry, low-humidity environments.
- Treat: isolate the plant. Increase humidity slightly (mites hate it). Insecticidal soap or
  neem oil, undersides of leaves, repeat every 3–5 days for 2–3 cycles to catch new hatchlings
  (eggs survive one spray). Predatory mites (Phytoseiulus persimilis) are the most effective
  non-chemical option if infestation is caught early.
- Avoid spraying oils on open flowers in bloom — treat in veg/early flower, or use only on
  foliage away from buds.

## Thrips
- Look: silvery/white streaks or patches on leaves, tiny black fecal specks, distorted new
  growth. Adults are tiny (1–2mm), narrow, and fly when disturbed.
- Treat: yellow/blue sticky traps to catch adults and monitor population. Insecticidal soap,
  spinosad, or neem for larvae. Predatory mites or lacewings for biological control.

## Fungus gnats
- Look: small dark flies hovering near soil surface, larvae are tiny white worms in the top
  layer of wet soil. Adults are mostly a nuisance; larvae can damage young roots.
- Cause: almost always **overwatering** / soil staying wet too long.
- Treat: let the top 2–3cm of soil dry out between waterings (this alone often solves it).
  Sticky traps for adults. BTI (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis, "mosquito bits") as a
  soil drench targets larvae specifically and is safe around roots.

## Aphids
- Look: small (1–2mm) pear-shaped insects, often clustered on new growth and stems, sticky
  "honeydew" residue, sometimes ants tending them.
- Treat: strong water spray to knock them off, insecticidal soap or neem for remaining
  population, ladybugs/lacewings as biological control.

## Powdery mildew (PM)
- Look: white, powdery, flour-like patches on leaf surfaces, spreading fast in still, humid
  air with poor airflow.
- Cause: high humidity + poor air circulation, especially in dense canopies.
- Treat: improve airflow immediately (fans, defoliation of affected leaves), reduce humidity.
  Potassium bicarbonate spray or diluted milk spray for mild cases on foliage (never on open
  flowers). Remove and discard infected leaves — don't compost them near the grow.
- Prevention matters more than cure here: keep humidity below ~55% in flower and maintain
  airflow through the canopy.

## Botrytis (bud rot / grey mold)
- Look: buds turn brown/grey and mushy from the inside out, grey fuzzy mold visible when the
  bud is pulled apart, often starts in the dense center of large colas.
- Cause: high humidity combined with dense bud structure and poor airflow, very common in
  humid climates during flowering.
- Treat: there is no effective in-season treatment once it's inside a bud — remove and
  discard affected buds immediately to stop spread to neighboring flowers. Prevention is the
  only real fix: lower humidity in flower (aim below 50% in late flower), increase airflow,
  and inspect dense colas regularly during the last few weeks.

## General environment-driven prevention
Most pest and disease pressure traces back to environment, not bad luck:
- Spider mites → too hot/dry.
- Fungus gnats → too wet.
- Powdery mildew / botrytis → too humid with poor airflow.
Fixing the underlying environment (see stage environment targets) prevents most recurrences —
treating the symptom without fixing the environment means it comes back.
