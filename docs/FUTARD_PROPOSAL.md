## One line

The journal that makes a grow checkable. This raise operates the desk and a creators fund. Crop economics, if they ever exist, are a later SPV — not this token.

---

## The problem

By week nine, nobody can reconstruct week two. Watering lives in someone’s head, photos in a camera roll, stages in a chat. When a stranger wants to back a plant, they are asked to trust a vibe.

Marketplaces that skip the trail mint-and-list the same day. Adopters cannot open the care log. Growers who actually document the grow get nothing for keeping it honest.

---

## Our solution

**Journal first.** Watering, feeding, stages, photos on one plant. Coach and reminders read the same log. Free, no wallet.

**Optional proof.** Seal a stage → mint queue → one plant NFT on Solana. Seed mint is not gated on 14 days.

**Market only after a trail.** Listing needs 14 elapsed days and care on at least half those days (`createdAt`, not backdated `date`). Instant sale or adopt-stake (50/50 care lock).

**Creators fund (this raise).** Pay growers who keep that trail alive, fund the inputs for their next crop, and co-fund the equipment that raises their ceiling. The last two return to the fund when they sell, so the same principal reaches the next grower.

Live desk: [growto.live](https://growto.live). Stories: [journal.growto.live](https://journal.growto.live). Devnet today. Test assets have no monetary value. On-chain is not a physical plant.

## The economy (designed Mainnet — counsel before go-live)

Journal stays **free**. Growtoo does not pay the year with a paywall. After this raise, further development is meant to come from **desk fees**, not from printing a coupon on the Labs token.

### How the desk earns (Labs → further development)

Planning take, not a live Mainnet fee and not a return promise. Exact percents wait for counsel.

1. **Listing / escrow fee — 8% of settled ask (USDC).** Charged when an Instant sale or adopt-stake settles. Goes to the Labs treasury. Funds audit, Mainnet, coach, hosting — the work above the $8k/mo cap still needs a proposal.
2. **Creators fund is not profit.** Trail stipends go out. Field advances come back when the grower sells. Revolving principal, not a yield product for token holders.
3. **Raise II origination (later).** If a harvest SPV ever exists: a disclosed cut of that raise (Jurassic used 10% USDC + 5% of that SPV’s supply — numbers for counsel, not a live promise). Labs originates; SPV holders take crop risk.

No subscription on the journal. No harvest inventory. No “token holders get 20% of every plant.”

### Worked example — $400 listing (illustration)

| Slice | USD | Who |
|-------|-----|-----|
| Ask | 400 | Adopter pays at purchase |
| Labs fee 8% | 32 | Treasury → development |
| Remainder | 368 | Split by offer type |

**Instant sale.** Grower receives $368 (minus any outstanding fund claim, which returns to the creators fund first). Adopter receives the plant NFT and the public trail immediately.

**Adopt-stake (as the product already specifies).** Adopter pays $400 now. NFT moves to the adopter. Grower receives 50% of the remainder on settle ($184). $184 stays locked in care escrow. Monthly care through the harvest-claim path releases the locked half to the **grower**. If that care path fails validation, the locked half **refunds to the adopter**. Physical harvest shipping stays mocked until Raise II.

If that grower had a **$100 crop-expansion advance** outstanding, it is repaid from the grower’s proceeds after the Labs fee, before the 50/50 split — $100 back to the creators fund. The claw never exceeds 30% of the post-fee remainder, so a settle always pays the grower something.

### How adopters get value (not ICO yield)

Adopters on the desk are **not** the same people as Futard ICO buyers, unless they also buy this token.

| Offer | Adopter pays | Adopter holds | Where “earn” can appear | Where it cannot |
|-------|--------------|---------------|-------------------------|-----------------|
| Instant sale | Full ask | Plant NFT + journal trail | Follow the grow; resell the NFT if a secondary exists | No harvest title, no Labs dividend |
| Adopt-stake | Full ask now | Plant NFT + trail; 50% of remainder locked | Locked half **refunds to the adopter** if monthly care fails; NFT is theirs | Locked half is **not** adopter yield if the grower keeps logging — it unlocks to the grower |
| Labs token (ICO) | USDC into Futard | Governance + treasury | Desk fees accrue to treasury under the cap; futarchy decides spend | No APY, no plant cashflow |
| Raise II SPV | Separate raise | That batch’s economics | Only here | Not this page |

Copy rule on the Futard page: **“You can watch this plant’s trail”** beats APY language.

## Creators fund

The fund is the part of growtoo a grower can feel. It does three things: **pay for a kept trail**, **fund the next crop**, and **co-fund equipment** — all gated on the same coverage math the Market uses (14 elapsed days, ≥50% watering/feeding days on `createdAt`). Fake-log farms do not get paid.

Two of the three windows are **recoverable**: money comes back when the grower sells, so the same principal funds the next grower. That is the difference between a fund and a giveaway.

### The three windows

| Window | Type | Ticket | Gate | Repayment |
|--------|------|--------|------|-----------|
| **1 · Trail reward** | Grant (not recovered) | $30–60 / month | Qualifying trail that month; per-grower monthly cap | None — this is pay for documentation |
| **2 · Crop expansion** | Recoverable advance | $300–1,500 | One completed 14-day trail + receipts for inputs | Returns on listing settle, 1.0× principal, no interest |
| **3 · Equipment co-invest** | Recoverable advance | $800–3,000 | Two completed trails + a quote; one ticket per cycle | Returns across settles, 1.0× principal, review at 3 cycles |

No interest, no revenue share, no lien by default. Counsel signs the terms before the first dollar (see the legal bucket) so window 2 and 3 are recoverable grants, not an unlicensed loan book.

### 1 · Trail reward — pay for keeping the log

Tier by **consecutive qualifying months**, not by XP or rank (rank includes mints; we do not pay for mints).

| Consecutive qualifying months | USD / month |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | $30 |
| 4–6 | $45 |
| 7–12 | $60 |

Cap **$60 per grower per month**, whatever the plant count. Miss the coverage gate and the tier resets to month 1 — the reward tracks care, not seniority.

### 2 · Crop expansion — fund the next crop, not a land purchase

Documented inputs only: seeds, soil, nutrients, pots, netting, irrigation line, small consumables. Receipts or a quote, photographed into the journal against a plant or desk.

- Paid to the grower, not to a vendor the fund picks.
- Repaid from listing settles at **1.0× principal**.
- The fund takes at most **30% of one settle**, so a grower is never zeroed on a sale.
- Never buys biomass, never buys land, never takes a share of the crop.

### 3 · Equipment co-invest — the thing that actually raises a grower's ceiling

Tent, LED, extraction / dryer, fans and filters, sensors, timers, a small irrigation controller. Bigger ticket, longer payback, and the reason a documented grower can go from a few plants to a room.

- **The grower owns the equipment.** The fund holds a recoverable claim, not title and not a repossession right.
- Repaid across settles under the same **30% of a settle** cap, up to **three harvest cycles**, then the position goes to review.
- One open equipment ticket per grower at a time. A second needs the first repaid or a proposal.
- Sensor-shaped purchases are preferred where they make the trail harder to fake — that is product value, not just capex.

### Repayment order on a settle

Labs fee → equipment claim → crop advance → grower (then the 50/50 split if it is adopt-stake). Fund claw is capped at 30% of the post-fee remainder.

### Sizing — $74,000 at the full take

| Slice | USD | Recoverable? | Rough reach |
|-------|-----|--------------|-------------|
| Trail rewards | $24,000 | No | ~$2,000 / month → roughly 40 growers at an average $45 |
| Crop expansion | $30,000 | Yes | ~40 tickets at an average $750 before any recycling |
| Equipment co-invest | $20,000 | Yes | ~10 units at an average $2,000 |

**Why a fund beats a one-off payout:** $50,000 of the $74,000 is recoverable. If that principal turns roughly 1.5× over 18 months, the fund puts **~$99,000** of support in front of growers from a $74,000 bucket — and the equipment stays with the grower either way. Turn rate is an assumption, not a promise; a cycle that does not sell simply slows the next draw.

### Guardrails

- **Concentration:** no grower holds more than **10%** of recoverable capital (~$5,000 combined across windows 2 and 3).
- **Monthly draw limit** on the bucket so $74,000 cannot leave in week one.
- **Dead trail = frozen grower:** rewards stop and no new advance is issued while the coverage gate fails. Existing claims stay as claims.
- **No pay per log.** Paying by entry buys fake `createdAt` farms; the coverage math is the product.
- **Never pays for mints or listings** — only for documented care, inputs, and equipment.
- **Write-offs are public:** forgiveness of a claim needs a futarchy proposal and shows up in the decision log, not a silent delete.

### Governance — rules pay, the market decides

The fund is designed so **no human decides who gets the monthly reward**. A grower who passes the coverage gate is owed the reward by rule; the community governs the *rules and the big tickets*, not the individual payments.

That split matters because the real question a Futard buyer has is “why won’t the founder just pay his friends?” The answer has to be mechanical, not a promise.

| Decision | How it is made | Why that way |
|----------|----------------|--------------|
| Does this trail qualify this month? | **Automatic** — coverage math (14 days, ≥50% care days on `createdAt`) | Objective and cheap. No discretion to capture. |
| Is the monthly claim list honest? | Public list + **72-hour challenge window**. Anyone can flag a trail; flagged claims go to review, the rest pay out | Community as fraud exception handler, not as paymaster |
| Reward rate, tier schedule, monthly envelope | **Futarchy proposal** | Real-money policy belongs to the market |
| Crop advance (≤ $1,500) | Rule + published caps, executed by the operator, logged | Too small to vote on each time |
| Equipment ticket ($800–3,000) | **Individual proposal** the market prices | Lumpy, judgment, concentration risk |
| Forgiveness of a claim | **Futarchy proposal** | Silent write-offs are how the fund dies |
| Routing fee points back into the fund | **Futarchy proposal** | Treasury policy |

**Oversubscription is pro-rata, not first-come.** The monthly envelope is fixed. If qualifying claims exceed it, every grower is paid the same fraction. The fund cannot be drained by whoever clicks fastest, and the bucket cannot be overspent by construction.

**Not token-weighted voting on people.** Whales do not pick which growers farm. Individual names only appear in a proposal for a large equipment ticket, and only for a grower who already has a public journal slug. No emails, no Firebase uids in a proposal — same scrubbing rule as the public tape.

#### Decentralisation ladder (say the stage, not the ambition)

| Stage | Who computes coverage | Who pays | Honest label |
|-------|----------------------|----------|--------------|
| **0 · Today (Devnet)** | Operator, off-chain in Firebase | Nobody — test assets | Operator-reviewed. This is the current trust model. |
| **1 · Post-raise, pre-audit** | Operator, off-chain, **published** claim list + challenge window | Operator executes; every payment in the decision log | Rule-based and auditable. Still one signer. |
| **2 · After attestation hardening + audit** | Coverage proof published per plant-month | **Permissionless claim** against the fund program; envelope enforced in code | Automatic. Operator cannot skip or favour a grower. |
| **3 · Steady state** | Same | Same | Parameters live only in futarchy; operator keeps a disclosed emergency pause, nothing else. |

Stage 2 is what “decentralised fund” actually means, and it is **not free**: it needs the attestation line and the fund program inside the same audit scope as the Market escrow. If it does not fit that scope, it waits for a proposal rather than shipping unaudited money.

Do not write “decentralised, community-governed fund” on the Futard page while sitting at Stage 1. Write the ladder. The board rewards mechanisms it can check, and a stage you actually occupy is stronger than a claim a reader can falsify.
