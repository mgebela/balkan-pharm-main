02 · Where it starts

A good season nobody can repeat.

This is the problem in its ordinary form. Not a market thesis — a person standing in front of a plant that did brilliantly last year, trying to remember what they did differently.

The notes scatter

A notebook by the tent, photos in the camera roll, feeding schedule in someone's head

The good year fades

By next spring the detail that mattered is the detail nobody wrote down

And nobody else can check

Which is fine between friends, and useless the moment money is involved

Start here and you build a journal. Start at the token and you build a certificate nobody trusts.

03 · The decision

Build the boring half first. On purpose.

The obvious move with a blockchain grant is to ship the token and add the product later. We did the opposite, and it is the decision everything else rests on.

1 The journal works with no wallet and no crypto knowledge — a grower can use it for a whole season and never see the word Solana
2 That means the care log exists before anyone has a reason to fake it
3 The chain switches on only when a grower opts in — and then it has something real underneath it

It cost us months before anything was on-chain. It also means the thing on-chain is worth something.

04 · Chapter one · M1

The first wallet attached to a real plant.

M1 was the foundation: Solflare connect, a Firebase account tied to a Solana pubkey, and the token and seed collection deployed to devnet. Unglamorous, and the moment the two halves of the product met for the first time.

What we shipped

Wallet connect in the Adopt module · Firebase user ↔ pubkey link · $GROWTOO SPL mint and a Metaplex seed collection, both live on devnet

What it taught us

A grower does not want a wallet. They want their plant to still be theirs. Everything we built after this hides the wallet until the moment it earns its place.

05 · Chapter two · M2

A seed that exists in two places at once.

M2 was the first time something in the soil had a counterpart on a public ledger — and the first time the metadata had to carry enough to mean anything.

What we shipped

13 seed mints on devnet against a 10+ target · metadata on Arweave, every record carrying strain, batch and the id of the journal plant it belongs to

What it taught us

The link back to the plant is the whole value. A token that only knows its own name is a collectible; one that points at a documented plant is evidence.

Anyone can check all thirteen on Solscan right now. That was the point of doing it this way.

06 · Chapter three · M3

Making the care count for something.

M3 is where the idea stopped being a journal with a token bolted on. Growth became something you earn by logging, and the reward followed the work.

1 Sealing a stage updates the NFT and mints the grower's $GROWTOO reward
2 Sealing is gated by the journal — link the plant, log the stage, log watering, log feeding
3 Each completed step carries the ids of the entries that prove it, so the requirement can be audited later

This is the part we are proudest of. You cannot buy your way past it, skip it, or pay to skip it. You water the plant and you write it down.

07 · Chapter four · M4

We were asked for list and swap. We built an escrow.

M4 asked for a simple marketplace and public docs. Partway in it became obvious that a custodial middle step was the weakest link in a product whose entire pitch is “don't take our word for it”.

What we shipped

Two tradeable asset types · public RWA architecture and redemption docs · and an on-chain Anchor escrow that moves the plant and the payment in one transaction

Why we went further

A hot wallet in the middle can settle one side and drop the other. We had spent three milestones arguing that evidence should not require trust — the market had to hold to the same standard.

08 · What the build taught us

The best week was the week we deleted things.

Four milestones of shipping produces a lot of surface. The most useful decisions of the whole project were the ones that took surface away.

Four tabs, thumb-reachable

We collapsed the whole app to Journal, Log, Coach and Market. Growers use this standing up, one-handed, in a tent

Say the real reason

Every failure names its own cause. A grower who can't tell why something broke stops trusting the log

Nothing on trust

Where we could remove a step that needed believing us, we removed it — even when it was more work

09 · Who it is for

Three growers, one product.

We modelled the app on three real Croatian growing situations, and tested against all of them. If it only works for one, it is a hobby tool.

Zagreb

Five plants in a closet

Zadar County

A tent, and Adriatic humidity

Osijek · Baranja

Five hectares and a field daybook

The closet grower and the field operation need the same evidence trail. They just need very different amounts of help writing it.
